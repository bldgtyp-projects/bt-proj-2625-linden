#### Recommendation

Pursue **Phius ZERO 2024** certification, using the recommended variant as the basis of design. The building meets
all of the Phius heating, cooling, and airtightness requirements without unusual construction: the upgrades beyond
the current drawings are better windows, more insulation at the basement and flat roof, exterior shading, a
high-efficiency ERV system, and careful airtightness detailing. Solar panels then close the remaining source energy
gap. Certification adds independent verification that the house is designed and built as modeled.

#### The two Phius options shown

**Phius CORE** is Phius's main certification. Its heating and cooling limits are calculated for each project from
its climate, size, and occupancy. For this house, the Phius Criteria Calculator sets an annual heating demand of
10.9 kBtu/ft², an annual cooling demand of 7.7 kBtu/ft², a peak heating load of 9.4 Btu/hr·ft², a peak cooling load
of 3.5 Btu/hr·ft², and a net source energy limit of 6,550 kWh per person per year. Airtightness must be 0.06 cfm50
per ft² of envelope or better. The recommended building reaches a heating demand of about 9.9 kBtu/ft² per year, a
cooling demand of about 3.3, a peak heating load of about 7.2, and a peak cooling load of about 2.3, all within the
limits. Without solar panels, its source energy is about 7,100 kWh per person, about 9% over the limit; Phius CORE
credits part of the output of on-site solar panels, and panels producing about 3,700 kWh/yr bring it to about 6,000.

**Phius ZERO** has the same requirements as Phius CORE, except that the building must also reach a net source energy
of zero or less. On-site solar panels count in full; off-site renewable energy, such as community solar or
long-term renewable energy contracts, can also be used, but receives reduced credit. The recommended variant reaches
net zero with on-site solar panels producing about 23,300 kWh/yr. As noted in the
[Executive Summary](/#what-the-results-show), this is a certification calculation using standard assumptions for
occupancy and household energy use, not a forecast of the home's actual energy use, and the final solar requirement
will depend on the final equipment selections.

  <span>
    The heating demand has about a 9% margin, and the airtightness is modeled right at the Phius limit, so
    certification depends on holding the line on the windows, shading, insulation, and airtightness as the design
    develops. Phius also requires the home to meet the ENERGY STAR, DOE Zero Energy Ready Home, and EPA Indoor
    airPLUS programs, and a Phius-approved Rater or Verifier must be hired before Design Certification is awarded.
    Certification will require a certification-grade WUFI Passive energy model, thermal-bridge calculations, window
    comfort and condensation checks, product data sheets, an independent blower-door test, ventilation
    commissioning, and on-site quality assurance inspections.
