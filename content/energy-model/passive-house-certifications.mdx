#### Recommendation

Pursue **PHI Low Energy Building** certification, using the recommended variant as the basis of design. It is the only
variant that meets all of the Low Energy Building requirements, and it does so without unusual construction: the
upgrades beyond the current drawings are better windows, more insulation at the basement and flat roof, and a tighter
airtightness target. Certification adds independent verification that the house is designed and built as modeled.

#### The two PHI options shown

**PHI Low Energy Building** is PHI's certification for buildings that come close to, but do not fully reach, the
Passive House standard. It requires a heating demand of about 9.5 kBtu/ft² per year (30 kWh/m²) or less,
airtightness of 1.0 ACH50 or better, and a renewable primary energy (PER) demand of 75 kWh/m² per year or less, along
with a cooling demand limit. The recommended variant meets all of these: a heating demand of about 9.2 kBtu/ft² per year, 0.6
ACH50, and a PER demand of about 49 kWh/m².

**PHI Classic** is the full Passive House standard. It requires a heating demand of about 4.8 kBtu/ft² per year (15 kWh/m²)
or less (or a peak heating load of 10 W/m² or less), airtightness of 0.6 ACH50 or better, and a
PER demand of 60 kWh/m² per year or less. The recommended variant already meets the airtightness and PER limits, but
its heating demand is about twice the Classic limit and its peak heating load (about 22 W/m²) is more than twice the
alternative limit. Reaching Classic would mean roughly halving the remaining heating demand, which for a house of
this size and glazing area would take substantially more insulation, window, and thermal-bridge upgrades. We have not
modeled a Classic variant, and do not consider it cost-effective for this project.
