In order to evaluate a building’s performance, [total annual energy consumption](#site-energy) is key, as shown above.
However, in addition to this top line figure, Phius certification requires the building to meet limits on its annual
heating and cooling demand and on its peak heating and cooling loads. These limits are calculated for each project
from its climate, size, and occupancy, and keep the building's heating and cooling needs low enough that a small,
simple system can keep it comfortable. Where the home fails to meet these targets is a clear indication that
improvements are possible.

Shown below are results for these assessment metrics, for each of the tested variants. The charts show whole-house
totals rather than values per square foot: for this house, the Phius heating demand limit of 10.9 kBtu/ft² per year
equals about 19,200 kWh per year, the cooling demand limit of 7.7 kBtu/ft² equals about 13,500 kWh, the peak heating
load limit of 9.4 Btu/hr·ft² equals about 16,500 W (56,400 Btu/h), and the peak cooling load limit of 3.5 Btu/hr·ft²
equals about 6,200 W.

The PH Windows variant is the first to meet all four limits, and every variant after it builds on that result.
The recommended Phius ZERO variant reaches a heating demand of about 9.9 kBtu/ft² per year against a limit of 10.9
(about a 9% margin), a peak heating load of about 7.2 Btu/hr·ft² against 9.4, a cooling demand of about 3.3
kBtu/ft² per year against 7.7, and a peak cooling load of about 2.3 Btu/hr·ft² against 3.5. Heating demand is the
tightest of the four, so it is the result to watch as the design develops.

Cooling demand rises by about 20% as the windows improve, because less of the summer solar gain escapes back out
through the glass. Exterior shading on the upper west and south windows more than offsets this; see the
[Summer Radiation](/windows/#summer-radiation) section.
