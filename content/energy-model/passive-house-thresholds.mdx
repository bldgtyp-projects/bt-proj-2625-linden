In order to evaluate a building’s performance, [total annual energy consumption](#site-energy) is key, as shown above. 
However, in addition to this top line figure the ‘Passive House’ framework suggests that the building 
should also meet additional heating and cooling annual energy demand performance targets. It is also 
useful to compare the peak-heating and peak-cooling loads to the recommended limits for Passive House 
buildings. While these limits are not required for certification in all cases, it is still good practice 
to attempt to meet them where possible. Where the home fails to meet these targets is a clear indication 
that improvements are possible.

Shown below are results for these assessment metrics, for each of the tested variants.

The recommended PHI Low Energy Building variant meets the heating and cooling demand limits, although its heating
demand is within about 3% of the limit. None of the variants meet the recommended peak cooling load of about 8.8 W/m2
(the recommended variant is about 12 W/m2). Cooling demand also rises slightly as the windows improve, because less
of the summer solar gain escapes through the glass. Both results point to the high solar gains through the east,
west, and upper south glazing, and support the exterior shading recommendations in the
[Summer Radiation](/windows/#summer-radiation) section.
