As shown in the results below, in order to assess the building performance we have tested the building in six distinct
configurations (variants). Each variant builds on the one before it:

1. **Code Min:** A version which meets but does not exceed the **<VarLink
hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.code_base_url" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /></VarLink>** code minimums for assembly
U-values, window U-values, airtightness (3.0 ACH50) and equipment efficiencies. This variant assumes a basic balanced
ventilation system with heat recovery, as required by the Boulder code, and that the building uses all-electric
appliances and equipment for heating, cooling, hot water and cooking.

2. **Insulation + Airtightness + ERV:** This variant uses the assembly R-values shown in the current design drawings
(approximately R-36 walls and R-52 sloped roof), with the basement wall and floor insulation increased to approximately
R-30 (3" exterior and 6" sub-slab graphite-enhanced EPS) and the flat roof increased to approximately R-76 (increasing
the polyiso above the roof deck from 2" to 4"), along with improved thermal-bridge detailing. It also tightens the
building to the Phius airtightness limit of 0.06 cfm50/ft² of envelope (about 1.0 ACH50) and replaces the basic
ventilation unit with two high-performance energy recovery ventilators (ERVs), at about 74% heat recovery and 65%
moisture recovery as rated by the Home Ventilation Institute (HVI). The windows remain at the code-minimum level.

3. **PH Windows:** This version replaces the code-minimum windows with best-in-class triple-pane windows (installed
U-value of approximately 0.14 Btu/hr-ft2-F, e.g. Smartwin), roughly halving the heat lost through the windows.

4. **+ Shading:** This version adds exterior shading to the upper-floor west and south windows to reduce summer solar
heat gain. The building in this variant meets all of the Phius heating, cooling, and airtightness limits, but not the
Phius source energy limit.

5. **Phius CORE:** The same building as the + Shading variant, with solar panels producing about 3,700 kWh/yr. This is
enough to meet the Phius CORE source energy limit of 6,550 kWh per person per year, so this variant meets all of the
Phius CORE certification criteria.

6. **Phius ZERO [RECOMMENDED]:** The same building again, with solar panels producing about 23,300 kWh/yr, enough to
offset all of the building's modeled source energy and meet the Phius ZERO certification criteria. The modeled heating
demand is about 9.9 kBtu/ft2 per year against the Phius limit of 10.9. Because this margin is modest (about 9%), design
changes to the windows, shading, insulation, or airtightness should be checked against the energy model.
