Phius does not prescribe a particular hot water system. Instead, the water heater's efficiency and the heat lost from
the piping and storage tank are calculated in the energy model, and certification requires documentation showing that
the system is built as modeled. Every fixture is also checked for how quickly it delivers hot water, so a compact
layout matters for both energy and certification.

#### Design recommendations

- **Planning:** prioritize compact plumbing layouts (stacked wet areas), with the water heater located as centrally as
  practical.
- **Hot water delivery:** Phius uses the DOE Zero Energy Ready Home limit of no more than 0.5 gallons of water in the
  piping between the hot water supply and each fixture, so that hot water arrives quickly without running water down
  the drain. Long branch runs to distant fixtures are the most common reason this is not met.
- **Branch piping:** keep branch piping as short as possible, and use the smallest pipe diameter suitable for the
  application.
- **Recirculation:** in a single-family home, Phius allows only demand-controlled recirculation, where the pump runs
  briefly when someone presses a button or a sensor detects someone at the fixture. Timer-based or continuously running
  recirculation is not permitted. See [Recirculation and Heat Recovery](#hot-water-recirculation).
- **Pipe insulation:** insulate all hot water piping, with insulation at least as thick as the pipe diameter (2x
  recommended), and continue the insulation through fittings, valves, and pipe hangers.
- **Waste-water stacks:** where code allows, use air admittance valves instead of roof-vented stacks. Roof-vented
  stacks inside the envelope lose heat through the stack effect; if they are needed, insulate the full vertical stack
  with 2" (50 mm) of insulation.

#### Phius requirements

- **Water heater:** a data sheet showing the rated efficiency (Uniform Energy Factor, UEF). A heat pump water heater
  inside the house is modeled with a reduced efficiency, because it draws heat from the house in winter. A split unit
  with its heat pump outdoors, such as the SanCO2, is modeled with a Phius calculator specific to the climate.
- **Storage tank:** a data sheet showing the tank's standby heat loss, or the tank size and insulation level.
- **Piping:** the length, diameter, and material of each hot water pipe, from the water heater to each fixture, are
  entered in the energy model and checked against the plumbing drawings.
- **Drain-water heat recovery:** units must be rated and labeled to CSA B55.1 to be credited in the energy model.
- **Pumps:** the power and controls of any recirculation pump.

#### Required submission documentation

Ensure that the plumbing plans and specifications include, at a minimum:

- **Piping** (hot water distribution and any recirculation):
  - Position and length of all piping, including the main runs, branches, and the final run to each fixture
  - Nominal pipe diameter
  - Pipe material (copper, PEX, etc.)
  - Insulation type and thickness
- **Equipment:**
  - Locations of the water heater, tanks, and pumps on the plumbing plans
  - Make, model, and size in the plumbing schedules
  - Data sheets for all equipment, including the water heater's rated efficiency and the tank's heat loss
  - Any pumps or circulators, and their controls
- **System description:** a brief description of the hot water system with schematic diagrams.

For more information, see the
[Phius Certification Guidebook](https://www.phius.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/Phius%20Certification%20Guidebook%20v25.1.0.pdf).
