title: "PHI Hot Water Requirements"
dek: "Hot water design and documentation needed for PHI certification."

PHI does not prescribe a particular hot water system. Instead, the system's losses are calculated in the energy model
(PHPP), and certification requires documentation showing that the system is built as modeled. Because the energy for
hot water is of a similar scale to the energy for heating in a Passive House, an efficient system matters. Note that
the PHPP uses a fixed hot water demand of 25 L (about 6.6 gallons) per person per day, so water-saving fixtures cannot
be credited; savings must come from the equipment, the piping, and heat recovery.

#### Design recommendations

- **Planning:** prioritize compact plumbing layouts (stacked wet areas), with the water heater located as centrally as
  practical.
- **Branch piping:** keep branch piping as short as possible, and use the smallest pipe diameter suitable for the
  application.
- **Circulation:** if recirculation is used, specify efficient pumps with demand controls to limit circulation times.
- **Pipe insulation:** insulate all hot water and circulation piping, with insulation at least as thick as the pipe
  diameter (2x recommended), and continue the insulation through fittings, valves, and pipe hangers.
- **Waste-water stacks:** where code allows, use air admittance valves instead of roof-vented stacks. Roof-vented
  stacks inside the envelope lose heat through the stack effect; if they are needed, insulate the full vertical stack
  with 2" (50 mm) of insulation.

#### PHI requirements

- **Pipe insulation evidence:** photographs of the insulation at fittings, valves, and pipe hangers must be taken
  during construction. Without them, the PHPP must assume the lowest insulation quality, which increases the modeled
  losses.
- **Water heater:** manufacturer data showing heating capacity and efficiency (COP) for at least three operating
  points. A heat pump water heater that draws indoor air is modeled with PHI's HPWH Tool, which also accounts for the
  cooling effect on the house.
- **Storage tank:** a product data sheet showing the tank's heat loss rate (W/K).
- **Pumps:** PHPP default values are used unless product data sheets show lower power consumption.
- **Drain-water heat recovery:** units must be PHI-certified or tested to CSA B55 (or equivalent) to be credited.

#### Required submission documentation

Ensure that the plumbing plans and specifications include, at a minimum:

- **Piping** (hot water distribution, circulation, and vented waste-water pipes):
  - Position and length of all piping
  - Nominal pipe diameter
  - Pipe material (copper, PEX, etc.)
  - Insulation type, thickness, and thermal conductivity, with material data sheets
- **Equipment:**
  - Locations of the water heater, tanks, and pumps on the plumbing plans
  - Make, model, and size in the plumbing schedules
  - Data sheets for all equipment, including the water heater's COP data and the tank's heat loss rate
  - Any pumps or circulators, and their controls
- **System description:** a brief description of the hot water system with schematic diagrams.
- **Photographs** of the installed pipe insulation, as described above.
