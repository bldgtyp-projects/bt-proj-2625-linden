#### Integrated heat pump water heater

For hot water generation we strongly recommend a high-performance electric heat pump water heater. One option is an
all-in-one (integrated) heat pump water heater such as those offered by
[Rheem](https://www.rheem.com/products/residential/water-heating/heat-pump-water-heaters/),
[State](https://www.statewaterheaters.com/products/residential/electric/proline/xe/heat-pump/), and others. In these
units, the heat pump is mounted directly on top of the tank, which makes them simpler to install and maintain than
'split' configurations. They run on electricity, avoiding combustion inside the house, and are very energy efficient.

Integrated units do have some drawbacks compared with split units such as the SanCO2 (below): they are noisier, need a
relatively large room to draw heat from, and cool the space around them. In most cases they cannot be installed in a
small enclosed closet without losing performance.

#### Split heat pump water heater (SanCO2)

Another option is a split system such as the SanCO2 heat pump water heater. The heat pump sits outdoors and moves heat
from the outdoor air to an indoor storage tank, giving very high efficiency and low energy use. It uses CO<sub>2</sub> as
its refrigerant, which has a much lower global-warming impact than typical refrigerants. Refrigerant leakage is a
significant driver of near-term warming, so low-impact refrigerants are an important part of reducing a building's
climate impact. Split units are also silent indoors and do not cool the indoor space.

These units are more expensive, however, and the water piping running to and from the outdoor unit must be carefully
detailed and installed to prevent freezing, particularly in Boulder's cold winters.

For more information refer to [ECO2 Systems](https://eco2waterheater.com/product-info/), the manufacturer of the
SanCO2.
