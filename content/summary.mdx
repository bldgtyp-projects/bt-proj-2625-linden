This report covers the design-phase energy modeling for the new single-family home in Boulder, Colorado. The energy
model was built from the drawings and models received on
<Var k="source_files.cad_files_received_date" />, and carries about 6,000 ft² of interior conditioned floor area
inside about 19,300 ft² of thermal envelope.

We tested six _variants_, from a Boulder energy code baseline up to a Phius ZERO building. **We recommend the Phius
ZERO variant**; the others show what each step contributes. Every variant includes a room-sealed wood stove,
modeled as Phius requires (see [Fireplaces](/mechanical/#fireplaces)). The Phius CORE and Phius ZERO variants are the same
building and differ only in the size of the solar array. The findings below drive the recommendations in the pages
that follow.

#### What the results show

1. **Insulation, airtightness, and ventilation are the foundation.** Together, the added insulation, tightening the
   building from 3.0 to about 1.0 ACH50, and a high-efficiency ventilation system cut the heating demand by about 26,200 kWh/yr and
   total energy use by about a third compared with the code baseline (from about 56,900 to 37,800 kWh/yr). The
   airtightness and ventilation steps have low material costs, but both need careful detailing and must be written into the
   construction documents.

2. **The windows are the biggest single lever.** Moving from code-minimum to Passive House-quality triple-pane windows cuts
   the heating demand by about another 15,900 kWh/yr. The better windows also keep the glass warm enough for comfort
   without the need for heating below the windows; the clerestory windows still need a confirmed U-value (see the
   recommendations below). See [Window Thermal Comfort](/windows/#window-thermal-comfort).

3. **Exterior shading offsets the higher cooling demand.** With the better windows, the house is better insulated
   and retains heat more effectively, which raises the cooling demand by about 20%. Exterior shading on the upper west and south windows offsets this additional cooling demand while improving
   occupant comfort. See
   [Summer Radiation](/windows/#summer-radiation).

4. **The Phius heating and cooling limits are met.** In the WUFI Passive certification model, the recommended
   building reaches a heating demand of about 9.9 kBtu/ft² per year against a Phius limit of 10.5, and a peak heating
   load of about 7.2 Btu/hr·ft² (about 43 kBtu/h, or 3.6 tons) against a limit of 7.7 (about 46 kBtu/h). Both are
   about 6% under their limits. The 43 kBtu/h figure gives a sense of the heating equipment required; the mechanical
   designer's load calculation will set the final sizes. Cooling demand and peak cooling load are well within their
   limits. The other figures in this report come from the PHPP design model, which gives slightly lower heating
   results (about 9.5 kBtu/ft² and 42 kBtu/h). In it, the building's total energy use is about 26,800 kWh/yr, about
   53% less than the code baseline.

5. **Solar is needed to meet the Phius source energy limit.** Without solar panels, the building's source energy is
   about 6,900 kWh per person per year, about 5% over the Phius CORE limit of 6,550. Solar panels producing about
   3,700 kWh/yr bring it to about 5,700 and meet Phius CORE. Panels producing about 23,300 kWh/yr offset all of the
   building's modeled source energy and meet Phius ZERO. Both solar figures include margin: the calculated minimums
   are about 1,100 kWh/yr for Phius CORE and about 20,600 kWh/yr for Phius ZERO.

   These are certification targets, not a forecast of the home's actual energy use. The Phius calculation uses
   standard assumptions for occupancy and typical household energy use, such as appliances, lighting, and
   electronics, and does not include extras like electric vehicle charging, a pool or hot tub, or snow melting. If
   the goal is for the solar panels to cover the household's real energy use, size the array for those additional
   loads as well.

#### What we recommend

- Use the **Phius ZERO** variant as the basis of design and pricing, whether pursuing certification or not. If the
  full solar array is not wanted, the same building with the smaller array meets Phius CORE. See
  [Passive House Certifications](/energy_model/#passive-house-certifications).
- Specify **Passive House-quality triple-pane windows** at an installed U-value of about 0.14 Btu/hr·ft²·°F, with
  non-metallic flashings and carefully developed installation details. See
  [Window Installation](/windows/#window-installation).
- Confirm that the **clerestory windows** have a rated whole-window U-value of about 0.14 Btu/hr·ft²·°F or lower, to
  meet the Phius window comfort requirement for their height. See [Window Thermal Comfort](/windows/#window-thermal-comfort).
- Increase the **wall insulation** to about R-44 with an average of 3" of exterior mineral wool insulation and an insulated 3-1/2" service
  cavity, increase the **basement wall and slab insulation** to about R-30, and rebuild the **flat roof** at about R-65 with 4" of polyiso above the roof deck, as shown in
  the [Recommended Assemblies](/building_envelope/#recommended-assemblies).
- Meet the Phius airtightness limit of **0.06 cfm50/ft² of envelope** (about 1.0 ACH50 for this building), and aim
  tighter for margin. Test mid-construction while the air barrier is still accessible. See
  [Building Airtightness](/building_envelope/#building-airtightness).
- Plan **two dedicated ERVs** with their own ducting (about 270 cfm normal, 355 cfm boost in total), each with an
  added frost-protection preheater. See [Mechanical](/mechanical/).
- Add **exterior shading** to the upper west and south windows.
- Plan for **solar panels producing about 23,300 kWh/yr** for Phius ZERO (about 3,700 kWh/yr for Phius CORE). Both
  figures include some margin. A solar contractor should size the system for the panels they would install, taking into account the
  roof orientations, shading, and any loads beyond the certification calculation. Final equipment selections, such
  as the heat pumps, water heater, ERVs, and appliances, and the hot water plumbing layout will change the calculated
  requirement, so the solar system
  should not be finalized until the energy model is finalized.
- Use a **heat pump water heater** with a compact plumbing layout. See [Hot Water](/hot-water/).
- Keep the house **all-electric**. Phius ZERO does not allow gas or propane appliances or fireplaces in the home
  (outdoor grills and a backup generator are allowed). If a gas range, dryer, or fireplace is wanted, Phius CORE is
  the fallback: it allows them, provided each is ready for a future electric replacement. See
  [Appliances and Venting](/mechanical/#appliances-and-venting).
- If a wood fire is wanted, use a **room-sealed wood stove** with sealed outdoor combustion air and a pressure safety
  switch, and test the room pressure at commissioning; otherwise, an electric fireplace is the simplest option. See
  [Fireplaces](/mechanical/#fireplaces).
- **Check design changes against the energy model**, particularly changes to the windows, shading, insulation,
  airtightness, or solar array.
