A high-performance window performs only as well as its installation. The window U-values used in this report are
installed values: they include the extra heat lost where the frame meets the wall (the installation thermal bridge, or
Ψ-install). A poorly detailed installation can add significantly to this heat loss and create cold spots around the
frame, undermining the comfort benefits described above and, in the worst case, leading to condensation and mold.

#### Installation principles

- **Position:** set the windows within the insulation layer of the wall, typically in line with the exterior
  sheathing, so that the exterior insulation can be continued up to the frame. Avoid recessing the windows deep into
  the wall or setting them outboard of the insulation.
- **Cover the frame:** where the frame design allows, lap the exterior insulation over the outer edge of the frame to
  reduce heat loss through it.
- **Airtight connection:** connect the frame to the interior air barrier (the Intello membrane) with a continuous
  airtight tape on all four sides. Use a vapor-open tape on the exterior to connect to the weather-resistive barrier,
  so the joint is tighter on the inside than on the outside and can dry outward.
- **Fill the gap:** insulate the gap between the frame and the rough opening with low-expansion foam or mineral wool.
  Do not rely on the foam as the air seal; the tapes provide that.
- **Support:** triple-pane windows are heavy. Support them as required by the manufacturer using blocks or brackets
  that do not create a continuous metal path through the insulation.
- **Drainage:** provide a sloped sill pan with a back dam that drains to the exterior of the weather-resistive barrier.

#### Non-metallic flashings

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Use non-metallic flashings at all windows and doors. Do not use metal sill pans, head flashings, or brackets that
    run through the insulation layer or touch the window frame.
  </span>
</div>

Metals such as aluminum and steel conduct heat hundreds to over a thousand times better than wood or insulation. A
metal sill pan or flashing that runs from the exterior to the window frame acts like a cooling fin: it bypasses the
insulation around the frame, increases the installation heat loss, and chills the interior edge of the sill, where
condensation is most likely. Metal sill pans are often the installer's default, so they should be specifically
excluded in the specifications and checked in the window shop drawings.

Use instead:

- PVC or fiberglass sill pans, or sill pans formed from flexible flashing membrane.
- Self-adhered membrane or fluid-applied flashings at the jambs and head.
- Metal only for exterior trim and drip edges that stay entirely outboard of the insulation and do not contact the
  window frame.

#### Coordination

PHI certification requires the Ψ-install value and a drawing of each installation condition (head, jamb, and sill for
each wall type), calculated to EN ISO 10077-2. We recommend that the architect develop these details early, coordinate
them with the window manufacturer's installation instructions, and send them to us for thermal modeling. Photographs of
the completed installation should be taken before the interior finishes are installed.
