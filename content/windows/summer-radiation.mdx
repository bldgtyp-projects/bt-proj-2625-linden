- Even taking into account the shading from trees and adjacent structures, high solar radiation is observed on the
  east and west facades and on the upper floors of the south facade. These unwanted solar gains will increase the
  cooling energy and reduce thermal comfort.
- The most radiation falls on the upper floors, which receive the least shading from the neighboring buildings and
  street trees.
- Overhangs work well for south-facing windows and doors. East- and west-facing glass is best shaded with operable
  exterior blinds or shades.
- The energy model assumes a solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) of 0.4 for all windows. A lower-SHGC glass on the west
  facade, where low afternoon sun is hardest to shade, would further reduce overheating risk.
