title: "Window Thermal Comfort"
dek: "Window performance needed for comfort near the glass in winter."
---

In any high-performance building, both the energy and occupant comfort impacts of the glazing must be carefully
assessed. For a building seeking Phius certification, this comfort impact must be evaluated as part of the
certification. This comfort evaluation looks at both the overall occupant comfort as a
result of air-temperature and relative-humidity but also the localized thermal discomfort caused by cold-surfaces and
drafts at the glazing surface.

In particular, the localized discomfort which results from a radiant temperature asymmetry (a difference in the
temperature of the surfaces surrounding the body) must be assessed in order to evaluate the possibility of eliminating
perimeter heating. If the radiant temperature asymmetry exceeds a certain level, a compensating heat source may be
required in order to offset the potential discomfort. Typically, this would mean a radiator of some form, installed
beneath the window.

Phius sets a maximum difference between the indoor air temperature and the inside surface of the window on a design
winter day. The taller the window, the stronger the cold downdraft it creates, so the allowed difference shrinks as
the height from the floor to the head of the window increases. For this site's design conditions [ an ASHRAE 99%
design temperature of 5.5°F (-14.7°C) ], the Phius criterion gives a maximum whole-window U-value of about
<Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F [ R-<Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_r_value" /> ]
for a window with its head 8 ft above the floor, about 0.16 for a 10 ft head, and about 0.13 for a window whose head
is about 12 ft or more above the floor, as in a double-height space. Phius checks each individual pane separately,
using the floor-to-head height of that pane, so the upper panes of tall window assemblies set the requirement.
Phius also requires a check that the glass and frames will not have condensation on them in winter.

The code-minimum windows in the first two variants (installed U-value of approximately 0.29 Btu/hr-ft2-F) do not meet
this criterion. The best-in-class windows in the PH Windows and later variants (installed U-value of approximately
<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F) meet it for windows with heads up to about 11 ft above
the floor.

The clerestory windows, with heads about 13.5 ft above the finished floor, fall under the strictest part of the criterion and
need a whole-window U-value of about 0.13 Btu/hr-ft2-F or lower, slightly better than the 0.14 assumed for the windows
overall. We recommend specifying the clerestory units to meet this, either with a higher-performance glass package or
by confirming that the selected product's rated whole-window U-value (at the standard NFRC size, which Phius accepts)
is 0.13 or lower. Phius also allows perimeter heating within 3 ft of the window as an alternative, but this is not
practical for windows this high. Windows in spaces that are not regularly occupied, such as a stair or hallway used
only in passing, are exempt from the criterion, so the requirement applies only to clerestories in rooms where people
spend time.

This is one of the main reasons we recommend these windows: with lower-performing windows,
heating below the larger windows may be needed to keep occupants comfortable.
