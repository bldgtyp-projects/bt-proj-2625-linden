dek: "The airtightness target and how to achieve it."
---

The primary role of airtightness is to protect the structure. In winter and the shoulder seasons, warm indoor air
leaking out through the envelope carries moisture into the walls and roof, where it can condense and lead to mold and
moisture damage.

Airtightness also has a direct effect on energy use and comfort: the more airtight the construction, the less heat is
lost in winter, the fewer cold drafts occupants feel, and the less the indoor air dries out in cold weather.

Code-minimum construction in most US states requires an airtightness of between 3 and 5 air changes per hour at 50 Pa
(ACH50). The <VarLink hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.code_airtightness_url" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /> </VarLink>
requires single-family homes to be blower-door tested at <Var k="narrative.energy_code.ach_limit" /> ACH@50Pa or
less (Section R402.4.1.2).

Phius certification requires an airtightness of **0.06 cfm50 per ft² of envelope area** or better, confirmed by a
whole-building blower-door test once construction is substantially complete. Phius measures air leakage per square
foot of building envelope rather than per unit of volume; for this house, the Phius limit corresponds to about
<Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH@50Pa, and it is the level assumed in the recommended variant.
Because the energy model sits right at the limit, we recommend that this project target better than the limit, about
0.04 cfm50/ft² (about 0.7 ACH@50Pa), so that the final test has margin. Improving airtightness to this low level is
one of the best ways to improve performance, increase comfort and durability, and reduce the risk of uncontrolled
wintertime air leakage.

We recommend a blower-door test mid-construction, once the air barrier is complete but still accessible, so that
leaks can be found and repaired before they are covered by finishes. The final test must be done with the windows,
doors, ERVs, exhaust fans, and range hood installed, and all penetrations sealed.
