dek: "The airtightness target and how to achieve it."
---

The primary role of airtightness is to protect the structure. In winter and the shoulder seasons, warm indoor air
leaking out through the envelope carries moisture into the walls and roof, where it can condense and lead to mold and
moisture damage.

Airtightness also has a direct effect on energy use and comfort: the more airtight the construction, the less heat is
lost in winter, the fewer cold drafts occupants feel, and the less the indoor air dries out in cold weather.

Code-minimum construction in most US states requires an airtightness of between 3 and 5 air changes per hour at 50 Pa
(ACH50). The <VarLink hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.code_airtightness_url" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /> </VarLink>
requires single-family homes to be blower-door tested at <Var k="narrative.energy_code.ach_limit" /> ACH@50Pa or
less (Section R402.4.1.2).

PHI Low Energy Building certification requires 1.0 ACH@50Pa or better. We recommend that this project target better
than <strong><Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH@50Pa</strong>, the level required for full Passive
House certification and the level assumed in the recommended variant. Improving airtightness to this low
level is one of the best ways to improve performance, increase comfort and durability, and reduce the risk of
uncontrolled wintertime air leakage.
