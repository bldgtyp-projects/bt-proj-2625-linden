#### Gas appliances

Phius ZERO does not allow fossil-fueled (natural gas or propane) appliances or equipment in the home. That rules out a
gas range or cooktop, gas clothes dryer, gas water heater, gas furnace or boiler, and gas fireplace. The only
exceptions are outdoor cooking, such as a propane grill, and a backup generator for power outages. Every variant in
this report, including the recommended Phius ZERO variant, is modeled as all-electric.

If gas appliances are wanted, the project would need to pursue **Phius CORE** instead of Phius ZERO. Phius CORE
allows them, provided each one is ready to be replaced by an electric equivalent later. For example, a gas range needs
a dedicated 240 V, 40 A circuit ending within 6 ft of it, a gas dryer needs a 240 V, 30 A circuit within 6 ft, and
any gas heating or water-heating equipment inside the house must be sealed and direct-vented, with space, power, and
a condensate drain provided for a future heat pump. A gas range would also need a larger range hood and makeup air,
as described below, and gas appliances would increase the building's modeled source energy, so the Phius CORE results
would need to be rechecked.

#### Kitchen venting

Kitchen venting should be provided at the lowest rate that satisfies regulations. For electric cooking, limit exhaust
hood airflow to 200-400 cfm maximum through the selection of
an appropriately sized hood. Above 400 cfm, the code requires a dedicated makeup air system (IRC M1503.6). The Home Ventilation Institute (HVI) specifies a minimum of 40 cfm and recommends 100 cfm
per linear foot of range. For effective kitchen venting, sizing and placement of the hood can be more important than the
air flow rate, as sizing and placement affect the capture efficiency of the hood. Hoods should be as low as practical,
extend beyond the range by approximately six inches at the sides and be as deep as possible without interfering with use
of the range.

#### Makeup air systems

In order to properly vent any exhaust air appliances (clothes dryers, kitchen hoods, etc.) in an air-tight home, a
dedicated makeup air system may be necessary. This is especially true of homes with combustion appliances (e.g. wood
stoves/fireplaces) located indoors. In this scenario, makeup air from an automatic makeup air fan is used to balance out
the exhausted air. For Phius certification, the makeup air needed for the range hood and clothes dryer is checked
with the Phius Intermittent Exhaust Allowance Estimator, so the hood and dryer should be selected early.

Note that for most cases we do NOT recommend this strategy as it is costly, complicated, and adds considerable
complexity to the envelope construction (insulation, air-sealing) and represents a significant energy penalty to
heat/cool the makeup air.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Wherever possible, the use of all-electric systems should be considered, and combustion appliances should be
    installed only in outdoor locations (porches, decks, etc.).
  </span>
</div>

Heating / Cooling of any outdoor makeup air may be needed once it enters the habitable space. Self-closing (magnetic)
dampers should be included on all ducting to prevent air infiltration / exfiltration when the appliances are not in use.
In order not to compromise occupant comfort when makeup air is being supplied to the home, a high output heating element
will need to be sized and installed to heat the incoming volume of air to a comfortable temperature.

If a makeup air system turns out to be necessary, for example to keep a wood stove room within its pressure limit,
there are several good self-modulating makeup air systems (MUAS) with matching makeup air heaters (MUAH):

- [Electro Industries](https://electromn.com/make-up-air-2/)
- [Neptronic](https://www.neptronic.com/cmu/)

#### Makeup air system commissioning

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Proper commissioning and continued maintenance of the makeup air system is critical to the safe operation of the
    home, particularly when combustion appliances are present.
  </span>
</div>

Several things should be kept in mind when configuring such a system:

1. Ensure airtightness at all envelope penetrations.
2. All ducting insulated with vapor-closed insulation (min 2") and vapor-closed tape all joints.
3. If ducting makeup air to the kitchen range, provide no more than 60% of the makeup air at the base of the range/oven.
