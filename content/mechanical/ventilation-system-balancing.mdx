For this building, we recommend a high-performance balanced ERV system with approximately 80% heat recovery, which will
provide excellent indoor air quality while minimizing energy consumption and occupant comfort issues. The energy model
is based on the <VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> <Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.type_name" /></VarLink>. The design
airflow (about 230 cfm at normal operation, 300 cfm at boost) is near the top of a single unit's range, so two units
may be needed to meet the airflow and distribution requirements. The units should be
outfitted with high-quality air filtration, MERV 13 or better, which will be critical to ensuring clean and healthy
indoor air. Additional filtration may also be desired to manage wildfire smoke.

The values used in this analysis are assumptions only. We recommend contacting both Brink and Zehnder for design
assistance. The selected manufacturer or their representative should be retained to fully design, specify, and balance
the system after installation. This is critical for proper operation and required for all high-performance buildings.

#### System balancing

Prior to occupancy, the fresh-air system should be tested and balanced to ensure good air mixing, adequate supply to
all living spaces, and compliance with the certification requirements. Some ERV vendors provide this balancing as part of their services; if
not, a third-party commissioning agent may be needed.
