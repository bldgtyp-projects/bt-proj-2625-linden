For this building, we recommend a high-performance balanced ERV system, which will provide excellent indoor air
quality while minimizing energy consumption and occupant comfort issues. The energy model is based on two
<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> <Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.type_name" /></VarLink> units.
The design airflow (about 230 cfm at normal operation, 300 cfm at boost) is more than a single unit can deliver, so
two units are needed, each carrying about half of the airflow. The units should be outfitted with high-quality air
filtration, MERV 13 or better, which will be critical to ensuring clean and healthy indoor air. Additional filtration
may also be desired to manage wildfire smoke.

Phius requires the energy model to use the ERV's performance as rated by the Home Ventilation Institute (HVI) at the
airflow each unit will actually run at, rounded up to the next tested airflow. At that rating, each unit recovers
about 74% of the heat and 65% of the moisture from the exhaust air. Manufacturers' own efficiency figures are often
higher because they are measured under different conditions, so they cannot be used where HVI data exists.

The values used in this analysis are design-stage assumptions. We recommend contacting both Brink and Zehnder for
design assistance. If a different unit is selected, its HVI-rated performance at the design airflow should be sent to
us so the energy model can be updated. The selected manufacturer or their representative should be retained to fully
design, specify, and balance the system after installation. This is critical for proper operation and required for
all high-performance buildings.

#### Frost protection

In very cold weather, the ERV's heat exchanger can freeze. The Flair 400 protects itself with a built-in electric
preheater and is rated for outdoor air down to -4°F (-20°C), just below the site's ASHRAE 99.6% winter design
temperature of
<Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_winter_design_temp_F" />°F (<Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_winter_design_temp_C" />°C).
Below about 5°F (-15°C), however, Brink recommends an additional preheater to keep the unit running in balance: once
the built-in preheater runs out of capacity, the unit switches to an unbalanced frost-protection mode.

We recommend adding Brink's optional eBus preheater to each ERV. It mounts in the outdoor-air duct ahead of the unit
and is controlled by the ERV. This keeps the ventilation balanced on the coldest nights, which matters for
certification testing and for any fireplace or other appliance that is sensitive to house pressure. Each preheater
needs a 230 V power connection, so coordinate its location and wiring with the electrical design, and confirm the
model and settings with Brink or their distributor.

#### System balancing

Prior to occupancy, the fresh-air system should be tested and balanced to ensure good air mixing, adequate supply to
all living spaces, and compliance with the certification requirements. Some ERV vendors provide this balancing as part of their services; if
not, a third-party commissioning agent may be needed.
